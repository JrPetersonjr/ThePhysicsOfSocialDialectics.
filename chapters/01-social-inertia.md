# Chapter 1: Social Inertia

Newton said an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an external force. People work the same way.

Ever notice how hard it is to change someone's mind? Or how a group keeps doing something even when everyone knows it's pointless? That's social inertia.

## The Basics

When you're in a routine—even a bad one—it takes effort to change direction. The same applies to:
- Conversations stuck in circles
- Organizations that won't adapt
- Friendships that have run their course but nobody ends them

## Why It Matters

Understanding social inertia helps you recognize when you're pushing against it versus working with it. You can't always overcome inertia, but you can redirect it.

## Examples

Think about:
- Family traditions that serve no purpose but continue anyway
- Meetings that could be emails but happen every week
- Arguments where both sides repeat the same points

That's inertia. The momentum of habit.

## Breaking It

To change direction:
1. Apply force (introduce something new)
2. Reduce friction (make change easier)
3. Wait for external disruption (life events)

More on this in later chapters.
